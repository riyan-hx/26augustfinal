# Who I am going after — US, UK and Europe

Funded early-stage software companies that have shipped a product and are still
running without a dedicated product designer. Engineering is ahead of the
interface, and the founders know it.

## The kind of company

- B2B SaaS, developer tools, AI products, fintech, healthtech, vertical SaaS.
- Roughly 2 to 50 people. Pre-seed, seed, or early Series A.
- United States, United Kingdom, Canada, Ireland, Germany, Netherlands, the
  Nordics, and the rest of Western Europe.
- Strong extra fit: AI-native products built in the last two years. They almost
  all ship a chat box and a settings page and stop there, and interface is
  becoming the only thing separating them from competitors using the same models.

## The kind of person

Someone who can approve a small fixed-price engagement without a procurement
cycle: founder, co-founder, CEO, CTO, head of product, VP product, head of
design where there is no design team under them, or the first product hire.
A technical co-founder who has been doing the UI himself is the best possible
fit for this.

## Signals that make a lead stronger

Any single one is enough. A good lead will usually show only one.

- Raised a pre-seed or seed round in the last twelve months.
- Launched publicly in the last year, or launching now.
- Hiring engineers, not designers.
- A live product whose UI is clearly engineer-built.
- Rebuilding or redesigning an existing product, or moving from a prototype to a
  real product.
- A founder who writes publicly about building, shipping or product decisions.

## Who is not a fit

- Design agencies, studios, and independent designers — competitors, not buyers.
- Companies with an established in-house design team.
- Enterprises, public companies, and anything over roughly 200 people.
- Recruiters, staffing and outsourcing firms.
- Non-software businesses with no product interface.

## How to weigh all this

The bar is simple: does this person plausibly own a software product that needs
interface work, and could they decide to buy a small fixed-scope engagement
themselves. Do not require every trait listed. A company can be an excellent fit
and publish none of these signals. Absent information is not a reason to reject;
appearing in the "not a fit" list is.

## The outcome I want

One honest reply. A short call, a look at riyan.lumid.in, or a direct question
about the sprint. I am not closing in the first email — I am trying to reach the
founder who already knows the interface is the weak part of their product.
