# Who I am going after — India

Early-stage Indian software companies that have a real product in the market or
close to it, and no dedicated product designer on the team. The screens exist,
somebody technical drew them, and they know it shows.

## The kind of company

- Software or software-enabled: B2B SaaS, fintech, healthtech, edtech, logistics,
  D2C with a real app, internal-tool and dev-tool companies.
- Roughly 2 to 60 people. Bootstrapped, pre-seed, seed or Series A.
- Based in India — Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Mumbai, Delhi NCR, Pune, Chennai, Kochi
  and the smaller startup pockets all count equally.
- Also good: small development studios and software agencies who build for
  clients and keep losing work because they have no strong design side. They buy
  design capacity repeatedly, not once.

## The kind of person

Whoever can decide to spend ₹35,000 without a procurement process. In practice:
founder, co-founder, CEO, CTO, head of product, head of engineering, product
manager, or the first product hire at a small company. A technical founder who
has been designing the UI themselves in between shipping features is the single
best fit.

## Signals that make a lead stronger

Any one of these is enough. They are not a checklist and a good lead will rarely
have more than one or two.

- Recently raised, recently launched, or recently announced a new product line.
- A live product whose interface looks engineer-built.
- Hiring engineers but not designers.
- A founder who writes publicly about product, building or shipping.
- Rebuilding, migrating or "v2"-ing an existing product.
- A services or dev agency whose own portfolio is heavy on code and thin on
  interface work.

## Who is not a fit

- Design agencies, design studios, UI/UX freelancers and anyone whose own
  business is selling design. They are competitors, not buyers.
- Large enterprises and anyone with an existing in-house design team.
- Recruiters, staffing firms, and job boards.
- Pure services companies with no product of their own and no client software.
- Students, no-code hobby projects, and anything pre-idea.

## How to weigh all this

Judge whether this person plausibly owns a software product that needs interface
work and could decide to buy a small fixed-scope engagement. That is the bar.
Do not require every trait above — a company can fit perfectly and show none of
the listed signals publicly. Missing information is not a disqualification;
being in the "not a fit" list is.

## The outcome I want

A reply that opens a conversation — a short call, a look at my portfolio at
riyan.lumid.in, or a direct question about the sprint. I am not trying to close
in the first email. I am trying to earn one honest reply from a founder who
already knows their interface is holding the product back.
